## Market Overview

The document/visual scanning market is large and competitive, dominated by free tools from Apple, Google, and Microsoft. However, the pivot to "Visual Capture" (photo → structured knowledge) positions CORTEX differently from generic scanners. The question is: does a niche exist between "scan a document" and "save a bookmark"?

## Competitive Landscape — Generic Scanners (Red Ocean)

| App | Platform | Price | Strength | Threat Level |
|-----|----------|-------|----------|-------------|
| **Apple Notes Scanner** | iOS (built-in) | Free | Zero friction, already on phone, Live Text OCR | 🔴 HIGH — free, pre-installed |
| **Google Lens** | Android + iOS | Free | AI-powered, text + object recognition, translation | 🔴 HIGH — free, massive AI |
| **Adobe Scan** | iOS + Android | Free (basic) / $9.99/mo (Acrobat) | Best color reproduction, PDF quality | 🟡 MED — free tier is good enough |
| **CamScanner** | iOS + Android | Free / $4.99-9.99/mo | Feature-rich (math solver, translate, OCR) | 🟡 MED — privacy concerns, ads |
| **Microsoft Lens** | RETIRED Jan 2026 | Was free | Whiteboard mode was best-in-class | ✅ OPPORTUNITY — Microsoft Lens retired! |
| **PDFgear Scan** | iOS + Android | Free | No ads, no subscription, Lens replacement | 🟡 MED — growing fast as Lens alternative |
| **Genius Scan** | iOS + Android | ~$21.99/yr | Batch scanning, cloud sync, OCR | 🟡 LOW — paid, niche |
| **Scanify PDF** | iOS + Android | Free | Privacy-first, AES-256 encryption | 🟡 LOW — newer |
| **Prizmo** | iOS | Paid | Best handwriting OCR, accessibility | 🟡 LOW — niche |

## Key Market Event: Microsoft Lens Retired (January 2026)

Microsoft officially retired Lens in January 2026. Scanning disabled by March 2026. This created a gap — millions of users who relied on Lens's whiteboard mode, business card scanning, and Office integration are looking for alternatives. Microsoft recommends OneDrive but it's not a dedicated scanner. **This is CORTEX's entry window.**

## Visual Capture Niche (CORTEX's Lane)

CORTEX isn't competing with generic document scanners. After the pivot, it targets **visual knowledge capture** — photo a whiteboard, conference slide, recipe card, wine label, business card, handwritten note → structured, searchable, tagged knowledge item.

**Closest competitors in the visual knowledge space:**

| App | What it does | Gap for CORTEX |
|-----|-------------|---------------|
| **Readwise** | Saves text highlights from books/articles | Text-only — no visual capture (this is why we pivoted away) |
| **Notion Web Clipper** | Saves web pages | Web-only — no camera capture |
| **Evernote** | Notes + camera scan + OCR | Bloated, expensive ($14.99/mo), declining user base |
| **Google Keep** | Quick notes + image OCR | Basic OCR, no structured tagging or knowledge graph |
| **Apple Notes** | Scan + Live Text | No structured knowledge organization |
| **Obsidian** | Local knowledge vault | No camera/OCR integration natively |

**The gap:** No app does "photo → OCR → structured knowledge item with tags and search" well on iOS. Evernote tried but became bloated. Apple Notes scans but doesn't structure. Google Keep captures but doesn't organize. CORTEX fills this gap if it stays focused.

## Positioning

**CORTEX = Visual Readwise.** Readwise captures text from digital reading. CORTEX captures knowledge from the physical world — whiteboards, slides, labels, cards, handwritten notes — and structures it into searchable, tagged items.

| Mid-range | CamScanner $4.99/mo, Adobe $9.99/mo | Pro $3.99/mo |

## Verdict: 🟡 VIABLE IF FOCUSED

The generic scanner market is 🔴 RED OCEAN (Apple Notes and Google Lens are free and pre-installed). But "visual knowledge capture" is a genuine niche. Microsoft Lens retiring creates a window. The risk is scope creep — if CORTEX becomes another scanner, it dies. If it stays "Visual Readwise," it has a lane.
